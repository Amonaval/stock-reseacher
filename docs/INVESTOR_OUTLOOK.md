# Investor Outlook

## What the investor actually needs

A serious investor usually does not lack information. The problem is turning too much information into a disciplined decision.

The product should therefore optimize for five outcomes:

1. **Find fewer, better ideas**
2. **Understand why a company deserves attention**
3. **See what can go wrong before committing capital**
4. **Know what evidence is still missing**
5. **Keep a living thesis after the initial research is complete**

## Investor questions the app must answer

For every candidate:

### Why is this company here?
- Which strategy found it?
- Which investor philosophy does it match?
- Which screen conditions matter most?

### Is the financial profile actually attractive?
- Is growth durable or temporary?
- Are returns on capital healthy?
- Is leverage acceptable for the business model?
- Does accounting profit convert to cash?
- Are margins improving, deteriorating, or cyclical?
- Is valuation reasonable relative to history, industry, and business quality?

### What is happening inside the business?
- What does the company actually sell?
- What are its important segments, customers, geographies, and cost drivers?
- What are the growth engines?
- What is the moat or competitive advantage?
- What can competitors or regulation disrupt?

### Can management be trusted?
- What did management previously promise?
- What actually happened?
- Has capital allocation created value?
- Are dilution, pledging, related-party transactions, governance, or disclosure quality concerns present?

### What could break the thesis?
- customer concentration
- supplier concentration
- debt/refinancing
- working-capital stress
- cyclicality
- execution risk
- capacity utilization
- regulatory risk
- technology disruption
- promoter/governance issues
- valuation compression

### Is there enough evidence to act?
The app must distinguish:
- attractive company with weak evidence
- mediocre company with excellent data coverage
- attractive thesis with major unresolved risks
- thoroughly researched company with resilient thesis

## What the investor should see first

Not twenty scores.

The default company view should answer:

**Current research state**

**Why it deserves attention**

**What we know**

**What worries us**

**What is still unknown**

**What would invalidate the thesis**

**What research happens next**

Advanced metrics and scoring should remain available underneath.

## Investor control

The investor must always be able to:
- edit strategies
- reduce or expand the candidate set
- manually include/exclude a company
- override an automated stage decision
- request additional research
- mark a risk as important
- change research depth
- save a strategy philosophy
- revisit prior research decisions

The system should record these choices as part of the research history.

## Long-term product outcome

The ideal experience is not "show me stock data."

It is:

> Learn how I invest, continuously search for companies matching that philosophy, research them like an analyst team, challenge the thesis, and show me only the decisions and evidence that deserve my attention.
