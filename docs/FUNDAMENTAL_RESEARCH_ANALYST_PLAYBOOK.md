# Fundamental Research Analyst Playbook

## Analyst mindset

The app should behave like a disciplined fundamental analyst, not a stock-tip generator.

The job is to build and test an investment thesis from evidence.

## Research order

### 1. Understand the business before interpreting the ratios
For each company establish:
- products/services
- revenue model
- segments
- geography
- customer base
- suppliers
- unit economics where available
- industry structure
- cyclicality

Ratios without business context can be misleading.

### 2. Reconstruct financial history
Review multi-period:
- revenue
- operating profit
- margins
- PAT
- EPS
- ROE / ROCE
- debt
- interest burden
- working capital
- operating cash flow
- free cash flow
- capex
- dilution/share count

Prefer trends and changes over single-period numbers.

### 3. Separate quality from growth
Ask:
- Is growth profitable?
- Is growth funded internally or through leverage/dilution?
- Does incremental capital earn attractive returns?
- Are margins sustainable?

### 4. Study cash conversion
Accounting profit is not enough.

Investigate:
- CFO vs PAT
- receivables
- inventory
- payables
- advances
- capex intensity
- free-cash-flow conversion

A recurring divergence between reported profit and cash generation is a research trigger.

### 5. Evaluate balance-sheet resilience
Debt metrics should be interpreted by sector and business model.

Review:
- debt/equity
- net debt
- interest coverage
- debt maturity/refinancing risk
- contingent liabilities
- guarantees
- working-capital borrowing
- promoter pledge

### 6. Evaluate management and capital allocation
Track:
- prior guidance vs delivery
- acquisitions
- buybacks
- dividends
- capex decisions
- dilution
- related-party transactions
- remuneration
- disclosure quality

Create a promise-versus-delivery ledger rather than relying on the latest management narrative.

### 7. Understand competitive advantage
Look for evidence of:
- pricing power
- distribution advantage
- cost advantage
- switching costs
- brand
- regulation/licensing
- scale
- network effects
- IP/process advantage

Do not label a company as having a moat merely because ROCE is high.

### 8. Research industry and external context
Company research must eventually include:
- industry structure
- market growth
- competitors
- input prices
- regulatory changes
- technology shifts
- customer demand

### 9. Build the Bull thesis
State:
- what is improving
- why growth can continue
- what the market may be underestimating
- catalysts
- evidence
- assumptions

### 10. Independently build the Bear thesis
Search specifically for:
- thesis breakers
- weak cash conversion
- governance risks
- concentration
- competition
- cyclicality
- execution failures
- unsustainable margins
- leverage
- capital allocation mistakes

The Bear researcher must not inherit the Bull conclusion.

### 11. Resolve contradictions
Where Bull and Bear interpretations conflict:
- identify the exact disputed assumption
- cite both sides' evidence
- classify as resolved, mixed, or unresolved
- generate a follow-up research task if unresolved

### 12. Value only after understanding the business
Valuation should not rescue a poor business thesis.

Later valuation work should consider:
- historical multiples
- peer multiples
- earnings/cash-flow scenarios
- growth assumptions
- margin assumptions
- capital intensity
- downside cases

## Required analyst output for every researched company

1. Business summary
2. Why it entered the research funnel
3. Financial trend summary
4. Quality/cash-flow assessment
5. Balance-sheet assessment
6. Management/capital-allocation observations
7. Growth drivers
8. Risks
9. Catalysts
10. Governance observations
11. Bull case
12. Bear case
13. Contradictions
14. Missing evidence
15. Thesis invalidation conditions
16. Research confidence
17. Next research action

## Analyst discipline

Never confuse:
- company quality with stock attractiveness
- cheap valuation with low risk
- management guidance with verified outcome
- high growth with durable growth
- screen match with investment conviction
- absence of evidence with absence of risk
