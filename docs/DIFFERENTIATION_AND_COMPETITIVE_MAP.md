# Differentiation and Competitive Map

## Competitive reality

The market already contains strong products for specific jobs:

- Screener.in: Indian company financial data, long financial history, screening, custom ratios, filings and alerts.
- Trendlyne: broad screening, stock scores, analyst estimates, research reports, alerts, portfolio analytics and document/data access.
- TIKR: global screening, deep financial history, transcripts, filings, analyst estimates, ownership and valuation tools.
- AlphaSense: professional-grade document/search workflows, generative research, transcript intelligence, due diligence and multi-document analysis.
- StockEdge: Indian stock discovery, fundamentals, technicals, scores and AI-assisted analysis.

The product should not attempt to win by recreating all of these features.

## Deliberate overlap

Roughly 30-35% overlap with established products is healthy and necessary. The app still needs baseline capabilities such as:

- screening
- company financial history
- ratios
- filings/documents
- research notes
- valuation inputs
- alerts/monitoring later

Those are table stakes.

## Differentiated layer

The remaining product should focus on a workflow most platforms do not fully combine:

### 1. Investor-philosophy learning
Learn the investor's historical screen behavior and convert it into explicit, editable strategy philosophies.

### 2. Autonomous execution with operator control
The system can run screening and research end-to-end, while exposing review gates for strategy edits, candidate selection, financial-stage overrides, and research-depth choices.

### 3. Progressive research budgeting
Do not research 500 companies equally.

Allocate deeper effort only when a company earns it through financial evidence and research quality.

### 4. Full decision provenance
For every company preserve:
- why it entered
- what the system researched
- what evidence was found
- what was missing
- why it advanced or paused
- what the user overrode

### 5. Research memory
Maintain a durable company research object that accumulates:
- historical financials
- source documents
- evidence
- management claims
- risks
- catalysts
- open questions
- thesis history

### 6. Promise-versus-delivery analysis
Track management claims over time and compare them with later outcomes.

### 7. Adversarial thesis research
Construct independent Bull and Bear cases, then use a neutral contradiction layer instead of generating a single persuasive narrative.

### 8. Evidence-aware uncertainty
The system should be able to say:

> This company looks financially attractive, but we do not yet have enough evidence to justify deeper conviction.

That is more useful than a high opaque score.

## Positioning

Not another stock screener.

Not another stock-score dashboard.

Not simply ChatGPT over annual reports.

The intended category is:

> **Personal AI Equity Research Operating System**

A system that learns how the investor thinks, continuously discovers ideas, performs progressively deeper fundamental research, challenges itself, and preserves an auditable investment-research memory.

## What we should not copy

Avoid competing primarily on:
- number of ratios
- number of charts
- generic AI chat
- generic stock score
- raw news volume
- brokerage target-price aggregation

Existing products already do these well.

## Product moat hypothesis

The strongest potential moat is the combination of:

**personal investing philosophy + workflow automation + evidence provenance + user overrides + longitudinal research memory + adversarial thesis testing**.

Each component exists elsewhere in some form. The product value comes from combining them into one investor-controlled workflow.
